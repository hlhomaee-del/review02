5–10 Minute Warm-Up

COB, EOB, Main Term & Subterm Sprint

A fast review before students complete a workplace claim task. Students must separate payer sequencing, claim outcome language, and ICD-10-CM index logic instead of guessing from familiar words.

Teacher note: This review is intentionally challenging. It forces students to distinguish who pays first, what the payer already decided, and how to search a diagnosis from main term to subterm before completing claim paperwork.

Page 1: Concept Sprint

Click each flashcard first. Then students test each other using the partner questions. No answer should be revealed until the person has spoken.

Partner Understanding Check

Directions: Partner A asks Questions 1, 3, and 5. Partner B asks Questions 2 and 4. The person answering must respond first. Then click Reveal Answer. After revealing, check whether your partner got it right. If not, the partner fixes the answer using the explanation. Then switch who speaks.

Question 1 — Partner A asks

What is COB really trying to determine before the claim is paid?

Answer first. Then reveal and judge.
Correct answer: COB determines which insurance plan is primary, secondary, or sometimes tertiary, so the claim is sent and paid in the correct order.
Good. Now move to Question 2. Partner B asks.
Question 2 — Partner B asks

What is the difference between an EOB and a bill?

Answer first. Then reveal and judge.
Correct answer: An EOB explains how the payer processed the claim. It is not itself the patient bill, even though it may show patient responsibility.
Good. Now move to Question 3. Partner A asks.
Question 3 — Partner A asks

In ICD-10-CM index searching, what do you look for first: the main term or the subterm?

Answer first. Then reveal and judge.
Correct answer: Start with the main term, usually the disease, condition, injury, or key noun. Then use subterms to narrow the code.
Good. Now move to Question 4. Partner B asks.
Question 4 — Partner B asks

What kind of details do subterms usually add?

Answer first. Then reveal and judge.
Correct answer: Subterms add details such as body site, laterality, acute or chronic status, cause, severity, complication, or encounter type.
Good. Now move to Question 5. Partner A asks.
Question 5 — Partner A asks

If the EOB shows deductible applied, does that mean the claim was denied?

Answer first. Then reveal and judge.
Correct answer: No. Deductible applied usually means the payer processed the claim and assigned the allowed amount to patient responsibility.
Good. Partner check complete. Now go to the Mini Challenge.

Page 2: Claim Challenge

Read the claim file, answer six questions, check each answer, then show results.

Mini Claim File

Patient: Marcus Allen, DOB 09/18/1976.

Coverage file: Active employer group plan through current job. Also listed: spouse's plan, active as dependent coverage.

COB clue: Patient is the employee/subscriber on his own plan and a dependent on spouse's plan.

Provider note: Patient seen for acute bronchitis with cough and chest congestion. Pneumonia was considered but not confirmed.

Index practice: For diagnosis lookup, start with the condition bronchitis, then narrow by acute.

Primary payer EOB: Allowed amount $180. Paid $120. Patient responsibility $60 copay/coinsurance. Remark: processed according to plan benefits.

Next billing action: Secondary claim may need the primary EOB details attached or electronically reported.

Do not code as confirmed

Pneumonia was only considered. It is not the confirmed diagnosis in this file.

Do not confuse

EOB language may show patient responsibility without meaning the claim was rejected or denied.
