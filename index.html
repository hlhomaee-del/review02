Harder 10-Minute Review

COB, EOB, Main Term & Subterm Challenge

Students practice the kind of thinking test designers expect: reading messy information, finding payer order, avoiding rule-out diagnoses, and choosing the correct ICD-10-CM lookup path without being handed the clue.

Teacher note: The mini case intentionally looks like real chart information: insurance details are scattered, the provider uses R/O language, and the index clue is not shown until students answer the lookup question.

Page 1: Flashcards

Students flip the cards first. The backside is fully hidden until the flip, so the answer will not show through the card.

Partner Check

Directions: Partner A asks Questions 1, 3, and 5. Partner B asks Questions 2 and 4. The answerer speaks first. Then reveal the answer, check if the partner was correct, and switch roles.

Question 1 — Partner A asks

When a provider writes R/O cancer in an outpatient note, should the biller code cancer as confirmed?

Answer first. Then reveal and judge.
Correct answer: No. R/O, suspected, possible, probable, questionable, and working diagnosis language is not coded as confirmed in outpatient coding.
Good. Now Partner B asks Question 2.
Question 2 — Partner B asks

What is COB trying to determine when a patient has more than one active plan?

Answer first. Then reveal and judge.
Correct answer: COB determines which payer is billed first, which payer is secondary, and whether another payer is payer of last resort.
Good. Now Partner A asks Question 3.
Question 3 — Partner A asks

How do you decide the ICD-10-CM main term when the note has several tempting words?

Answer first. Then reveal and judge.
Correct answer: Start with the actual condition or reason being coded, not a random adjective, body part, symptom, or procedure word.
Good. Now Partner B asks Question 4.
Question 4 — Partner B asks

What does an EOB explain after the primary payer processes a claim?

Answer first. Then reveal and judge.
Correct answer: It explains charges, allowed amount, payer payment, adjustments, denial reason if any, and what may remain for the patient or secondary payer.
Good. Now Partner A asks Question 5.
Question 5 — Partner A asks

Why is “in situ,” “uncertain behavior,” and “unspecified behavior” not the same thing?

Answer first. Then reveal and judge.
Correct answer: They describe different neoplasm behavior categories. Do not swap them. Use the behavior documented by the provider/pathology, then verify in the Tabular List.
Partner check complete. Now start the challenge.

Page 2: Hard Challenge

Read the note as if it came from a real chart. No clue is underlined at first. Evidence appears only after the student checks an answer.

Complex Provider + Registration Note

Patient: Maris Cole, DOB 06/18/1974. Outpatient GI follow-up after colonoscopy and pathology review.

Registration notes from intake and eligibility screens: Patient presented three coverage cards. The first card was issued through the patient’s current employer, Riverview Community College, and lists the patient as the employee/subscriber. A second commercial card is through the spouse’s union benefit plan and lists the patient as spouse/dependent. The state medical assistance portal also returned active eligibility for the service date. No Medicare entitlement appears on today’s eligibility check. A prior front-desk note says a claim was once sent directly to the state plan and returned with a third-party-liability message.

HPI: Patient reports several months of altered bowel pattern, intermittent dark stools, and fatigue. Outside lab showed iron-deficiency anemia and positive stool blood test. Family history includes colon cancer in a maternal uncle. Patient denies weight loss. Provider documents: R/O malignant neoplasm of ascending colon pending full clinical correlation.

Procedure history: Colonoscopy last week described a friable sessile lesion in the ascending colon with partial removal and biopsy. The note also mentions diverticulosis and internal hemorrhoids. No invasive carcinoma was stated in the colonoscopy report.

Pathology addendum reviewed today: Tubular adenomatous colonic neoplasm with high-grade dysplastic change. Margins indeterminate on fragmented specimen. The pathologist comments that the biologic behavior cannot be predicted from the submitted tissue; invasive carcinoma is not identified in the specimen.

A/P: Ascending colon neoplastic lesion after biopsy; behavior not predictable on specimen. R/O malignant transformation; refer to colorectal surgery. Continue iron therapy. Repeat CBC. Discussed likely need for definitive resection depending on surgical evaluation.

Primary payer response summary received: Billed charge $780. Allowed amount $420. Plan paid $300 after network adjustment. Remaining $120 is listed as member cost-share unless another payer is responsible under coordination rules. Adjustment code indicates contractual reduction, not a medical-necessity denial.
